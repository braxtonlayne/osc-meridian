### Reactors

The lore gives "8 installations â€” 5 ALPHA sites, 3 BETA sites". The three BETA sites chosen:

- **BETA-08** (Nevada operations / weapons oversight; cover "Nuclear Test Site Support"). Nuclear material movements and radiological workers are already normal under the cover, so fuel deliveries and reactor staff draw no notice. It is also the deepest BETA site, at 780 ft.
- **BETA-09** (Central military coordination; cover "STRATCOM Auxiliary Facility"). It carries backup command and communications loads that cannot depend on the grid, and it is the only BETA site on the Central Network, so it backs up ALPHA-03's feeder line.
- **BETA-03** (Southeast ops / biomedical; cover "CDC Secondary Research Complex"). ### Charter

Article I is verbatim from the lore. Articles II-VII are extended, in the same register. The Charter is the only place in MERIDIAN where rhetoric is permitted.

**Article I: Purpose** (source)

> Continuity is not survival. Continuity is purpose sustained beyond crisis. This infrastructure exists not to shelter us from the end, but to ensure the Republic emerges whole from whatever that end may be.

**Article II: Membership and Succession**

> The Council shall consist of eleven members, drawn from those offices of government and industry upon which the continuity of the Republic depends: four of Cabinet rank, three who direct the agencies charged with the nation's intelligence and security, two of senior military command, and two from the industries that arm and supply its defense. A seat belongs to the office it represents and passes with that office. When a member departs, the seat stands vacant not for want of a person but for want of a briefing; the successor shall be admitted within thirty days, and the departing member's copy of every Council record shall be destroyed before the successor's copy is issued. No member shall hold two seats, and no seat shall be held in absence.

**Article III: Authority and Revision**

> The Council is the sole authority of the Office of Strategic Continuity. It acts by a majority of its members present and voting, seven constituting a quorum. This Charter, the Facility Inventory, and every instrument the Council names as governing may be revised by a majority of the full Council and by no lesser body. Authority delegated by the Council is held in trust; it returns to the Council upon the Council's word, without notice and without cause shown.

**Article IV: Facilities and Custody**

> The facilities of the Office are held in custody for the nation and are the property of no department. Each shall stand under the command of an officer answerable to the Council through the chain established by directive, and each shall keep in ordinary times the readiness it will require in extraordinary ones. No facility shall be surrendered, disclosed or abandoned except by resolution of the Council. The cover under which a facility stands is part of that facility and shall be kept with the same care.

**Article V: Personnel and Compartmentation**

> Those who serve the Office serve within the tiers and compartments the Council establishes. Each shall know what the work before them requires, and no more; this is not distrust but design. Admission to a compartment is a grant of the Council and may be withdrawn as it was given. The Office shall account for every person in its service, wherever posted or however carried, and the obligations of that service do not lapse with posting, transfer or release.

**Article VI: Reconstitution**

> Upon the Council's determination that the ordinary institutions of government cannot continue, the Office shall execute the plans of reconstitution it maintains, receive into its facilities those designated essential, and sustain the functions of government until they can be restored in their proper form. The Council shall then sit without adjournment. Its first duty is restoration and not rule, and it shall relinquish each function as the lawful institution able to bear it is restored. The determination itself rests with the Council alone.

**Article VII: Secrecy and Records**

> The existence of the Office is the first of its secrets. Its governing records shall be kept on paper, in the number of copies the Council directs and in no other form, and each copy shall be accounted for until it is destroyed. No member, officer or employee shall disclose the Office, its facilities or its purpose to any person not admitted to it, under any compulsion. What is not written cannot be lost; what must be written shall be kept as though the Republic depended upon it, for it does.

**Pass two additions (wave 2C audit, I2).** New kinds within existing families: `OSC-TRN-DSP-NN` (dispatcher desk documents), `OSC-RSV-SIM-NN` (reactor training simulator), `OSC-ASR-CP-NN` (contingency register entries), `OSC-CNL-DEL-YY-NN` (record of a meeting held under delegated authority, Article III; one number per meeting). A directive's annex or card issued with it takes the next directive number (the drill site card is OSC-DIR-0434, issued with OSC-DIR-0433), never a suffix. Memorandum serials run in one series per year in order of first issue (about three a day in 2007, two a day in 2008); a memorandum keeps its first-issue number through later revisions, so a memo first issued in 2006 or 2007 keeps its 06- or 07- number when revised in 2008. Message serials are assigned on delivery from one network counter (about ten a day in May 2008), so a message dated before 19 MAY sits below MSG-08-04480. Renumbered in the audit: OSC-DIR-0433-B → OSC-DIR-0434; OSC-MEM-08-0244 → OSC-MEM-06-0291; OSC-MEM-08-0164 → OSC-MEM-07-0262; OSC-MEM-08-0252 → OSC-MEM-08-0152; OSC-MEM-08-0301 → OSC-MEM-08-0166; OSC-MEM-07-1342 → OSC-MEM-07-0851; OSC-MEM-02-0061 → OSC-MEM-02-0284; fourteen per-account login messages MSG-08-061xx/062xx/063xx → MSG-08-04341..04477 (mapping in `docs/narrative-2/accounts.md`).
