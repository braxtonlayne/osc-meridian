## 1. General

The basic standard is: :c[security.personnel.investigation] The supplement adds four focus areas to the single scope background investigation conducted for the subject's clearance. Each focus area is completed and recorded separately and adjudicated with the investigation as a whole. Personnel security protocols as a whole are set out in :x[OSC-ASR-PSEC]; terms are as defined in the Glossary (:x[OSC-SYS-GLO]).

| Area | Focus | Conducted by | Schedule |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Multi-generational family citizenship verification | PV Investigations Branch | A |
| B | Financial independence from foreign-connected entities | PV Investigations Branch, with Comptroller | B |
| C | Psychological assessment for ideological reliability | Medical Services | C |
| D | Social network mapping | PV Investigations Branch, with Office of Internal Assurance | D |

## 2. Area A: Multi-generational family citizenship verification

2.1. The subject completes Schedule A, listing parents, grandparents, siblings, spouse or cohabitant, the parents of the spouse or cohabitant, and children over the age of sixteen, with place and date of birth and the basis of citizenship for each.

2.2. Citizenship is verified from primary records for every person on Schedule A in the subject's direct line to the grandparents, and for the spouse or cohabitant and their parents. Naturalisation is verified from the naturalisation record and the date entered on Schedule A.

2.3. Where a primary record cannot be obtained, the investigator records the gap, the records searched and the reason the record is unavailable. A gap is not adverse in itself. Two or more gaps in the direct line are referred to the adjudicating office before the investigation continues.

2.4. Schedule A is updated by the subject within thirty days of any marriage, cohabitation, birth, adoption or change of citizenship in the family.

## 3. Area B: Financial independence from foreign-connected entities

3.1. A foreign-connected entity is any company, partnership, trust, fund or institution in which a foreign government or a foreign national holds ten per cent or more of the ownership or control, or which has received foreign government funding in the preceding five years.

3.2. The subject completes Schedule B, listing every account, holding, loan, pension and source of income of the subject and of the spouse or cohabitant, with the counterparty for each. The investigator verifies Schedule B against credit reports and, with the Comptroller, against the subject's pay record.

3.3. Holdings in a foreign-connected entity, other than through a broadly held fund, are to be divested before tier assignment. Debts owed to a foreign-connected entity are to be refinanced or discharged. The adjudicating office may allow a period for divestment of not more than ninety days.

3.4. Schedule B is renewed annually by the subject in the month of the subject's appointment.

## 4. Area C: Psychological assessment for ideological reliability

4.1. The assessment is conducted by Medical Services under a protocol issued to Tier 2. The subject is informed that an assessment will be made and of its date. The result is returned to the adjudicating office as a rating; the clinical record is held by Medical Services.

:::redact{tier=2 auth="MS" ref="OSC-PER-VET"}
4.2. The assessment comprises a structured interview of about four hours, two standardised personality inventories and a scenario questionnaire. The scenario questionnaire presents situations in which an instruction of the Office conflicts with a duty or loyalty owed elsewhere (to family, to a former service, to a public office, to a profession) and records the subject's stated course of action and reasons.

4.3. Reliability is rated on a five-point scale. A rating of 1 or 2 bars appointment. A rating of 3 permits appointment at Tier 4 only, or at Tier 3 with a reassessment after twelve months. Ratings are not disclosed to the subject.

4.4. Indicators recorded under this area include: stated primacy of a loyalty outside the Office; expressed objection, on grounds of principle, to secrecy in government; a history of public advocacy or of disclosures to the press or to a legislature; and inconsistency between interview and questionnaire answers on the same scenario.
:::

## 5. Area D: Social network mapping

5.1. The subject completes Schedule D, listing persons with whom the subject has regular personal contact, by name, occupation, citizenship and frequency of contact. The investigator extends and verifies Schedule D.

:::redact{tier=2 auth="OIA" ref="OSC-PER-VET"}
5.2. The map is extended to the second degree: for each person on Schedule D the investigator records that person's own regular contacts so far as they can be found from interview, correspondence, telephone and financial records, and public sources. Persons in journalism, legislative staff, law enforcement outside the Office, or any foreign government service are flagged wherever they appear in the map.

5.3. The completed map is passed to the Office of Internal Assurance, which keeps it current for the period of the subject's service from records available to it. The subject is not told that the map is kept current.

5.4. The map is retained for the duration of service and thereafter. Schedule D and the map are not destroyed on separation, transfer or change of category.
:::

## 6. Periodic reinvestigation

6.1. Personnel are reinvestigated every five years from tier assignment at Tiers 3 and 4, and every three years at Tier 2. Areas A and B are repeated in full. Areas C and D are repeated as the adjudicating office directs.

6.2. Reinvestigation is brought forward on a trigger. Triggers known to the subject are: a self-report under :x[OSC-DIR-0378]; a change of marital status or cohabitation; a financial difficulty reported on Schedule B; a facility transfer (:x[OSC-PER-TRF]).

:::redact{compartment=BRIAR auth="OIA" ref="OSC-PER-VET"}
6.3. Further triggers, applied by the Office of Internal Assurance without notice to the subject:

- a change of personnel category other than by posting;
- contact, other than in the course of duty, with a person whose category has changed other than by posting;
- access to an item under inquiry, or to a record of a contingency protocol, outside the subject's tasking;
- a pattern of searches or of access to withheld material in the workstation audit trail;
- an attempt to correspond with an addressee whose account is retained but not accepting mail;
- a referral by a supervisor at an interim or final probation review.

6.4. A reinvestigation brought forward under 6.3 is conducted by the Office of Internal Assurance and its result is not returned to Personnel & Vetting. Pending the result, the subject's personnel category is held by OIA under :x[OSC-DIR-0233].
:::

## 7. Adjudication

7.1. The Director, Personnel & Vetting adjudicates the investigation as a whole and records one of: APPROVED; APPROVED WITH CONDITIONS; NOT APPROVED. Conditions are stated in writing to the subject.

7.2. A subject not approved may ask once, within thirty days, for the decision to be reviewed by the Director. The review is on the record and the subject does not attend.

7.3. Investigation files are held by PV Investigations Branch, Level 7, ALPHA-01, for the duration of service and for fifty years thereafter.
