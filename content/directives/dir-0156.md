## Purpose

This directive sets the requirements for fire drills, the assignment of personnel to refuge chambers, the testing of smoke doors and dampers, and the control of hot work below grade.

## Applicability

This directive applies to all personnel at every staffed facility below the surface cover facility, and to contract staff working there.

Fire safety at the surface cover facility is the responsibility of the cover agency and is not governed by this directive.

## Policy

Below the surface cover facility, personnel do not evacuate to the surface in a fire. They go to the refuge chamber assigned to their level and remain there until released by the fire warden or the duty officer.

Each level has one or more refuge chambers with an independent air supply. Every person is assigned to one chamber by level of work and one by level of berthing. The assignment is posted at each stairhead and on the back of each berthing door.

Drills are held at these intervals: each level, once a quarter, unannounced to occupants; the whole facility, once a year, announced seven days in advance; and each refuge chamber, a door and air supply function check once a month by Facilities Engineering.

Surface cover facility staff conduct their own drills under cover agency procedures, on a different day from any drill below grade. Drills below grade are not announced at the surface.

Smoke doors are checked visually every week by the fire warden for the level, function-tested every month, and drop-tested once a year with the smoke control dampers on the same zone.

No hot work (welding, cutting, brazing, grinding) is done below grade without a hot work permit issued by the senior engineer and countersigned by the duty officer. A fire watch remains at the site for sixty minutes after the work ends.

Tobacco is not used below grade under any circumstances, as set out in :x[OSC-DIR-0480].

## Responsibilities

The Facility Commander appoints a fire warden and a deputy for each level and each watch, and publishes the roster with the duty roster.

The senior engineer at each facility keeps the refuge chamber register, schedules the monthly checks, issues hot work permits and keeps the smoke door test record.

Fire wardens carry out the weekly smoke door checks, account for the personnel of their level at the chamber during a drill or a fire, and report the count to the duty officer.

The duty officer starts unannounced drills, receives the counts, and decides when personnel are released from the chambers.

Each person knows their two assigned chambers, goes to the nearer one when the alarm sounds, and reports to the warden on arrival.

## Procedures

On the alarm, personnel stop work, secure classified material where it can be done in under thirty seconds, and go to the nearest assigned chamber by the marked route. Lifts are not used.

On arrival the warden takes the count by name against the level list. Persons not accounted for are reported to the duty officer at once, with the place they were last seen.

The chamber door is closed when the warden's count is complete or when smoke reaches the lobby, whichever is first. A person arriving after the door is closed is admitted through the lobby airlock.

The drill record states the level, the time from alarm to door closed, the count, and any person not accounted for. It is retained by the senior engineer with the refuge chamber register, and a copy is sent to the facility command section.

A drill that takes longer than four minutes from alarm to door closed on any level is repeated on that level within thirty days.

A real fire, a false alarm and a smoke door failure are each reported on :x[OSC-F-114] by the warden or the senior engineer before the end of the duty period.
