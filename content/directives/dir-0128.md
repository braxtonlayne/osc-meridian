## Purpose

This directive sets the replacement cycles for particulate pre-filters, final filters and gas-phase beds in air handling units (AHU), the conditions under which a filter bank may be opened, and the notice given to occupants of the zones affected.

## Applicability

This directive applies to Facilities Engineering personnel and contract maintenance staff working on air handling plant at any staffed facility, and to duty officers who approve work under paragraph 3.5.

It does not apply to plant at the surface cover facility, which is maintained under the cover agency's own arrangements.

## Policy

Each AHU is tagged with its zone number and filter cycle. The standard cycles are:

| Filter stage | Standard cycle | Change earlier at |
|---|---|---|
| Particulate pre-filter | 90 days | 1.0 in. w.g. differential pressure |
| Final (high-efficiency) filter | 24 months | 2.0 in. w.g. differential pressure |
| Gas-phase bed | 180 days | Failed breakthrough sample |

Units serving reactor spaces and units serving seal-capable zones are on shortened cycles: particulate pre-filters every 60 days and gas-phase beds every 120 days. Final filters in these units are tested at each pre-filter change.

Seal-capable zones at facilities are listed in Annex A. Seal-capable zones in tunnels and shaft houses are listed in Annex B. The annexes are released only to those who need them to plan the work.

No filter bank is opened in a zone whose seal state is SEALED or SEALED (DRILL). A change falling due in that period is made on the first working day after the state is lifted, and the deferral is recorded in the filter change log.

In a zone at CONDITIONED, a filter bank serving a seal-capable zone is opened only with the approval of the duty officer. The approval is recorded in the duty log with the unit number and the time of isolation.

Work is planned so that no zone loses more than one AHU at a time. Where a zone is served by a single unit, the interruption is not to exceed 60 minutes for a particulate change and 4 hours for a gas-phase change.

Spent gas-phase media from units serving seal-capable zones are bagged at the unit, labelled with the unit number and date, and handled as controlled waste. They are not placed in general waste at any facility.

## Responsibilities

The Chief Engineer, Facilities Engineering, owns this directive, approves deviations from the cycles in paragraph 3.1 and keeps Annexes A and B current.

The senior engineer at each facility issues the filter change schedule for the facility each quarter, posts it at the plant room and on MERIDIAN, and countersigns the filter change log.

Life support technicians carry out the work, record each change in the log before leaving the plant room, and give notice to occupants as required by paragraph 5.1.

Duty officers approve work under paragraph 3.5 and may postpone any change when the operational situation requires it.

## Procedures

Occupants of each zone affected are given not less than seventy-two hours' notice of a planned change, by MERIDIAN mail and by notice posted at the level control point. The notice states the units, the zones, the start and finish times in Zulu with local time in brackets, and the expected reduction in supply.

To change a filter bank:

1. Confirm the seal state of every zone the unit serves.
2. Isolate the unit at the local panel and attach the isolation tag.
3. Record the differential pressure across the bank before removal.
4. Remove the spent filters or media. Bag and label media from seal-capable zones at the unit.
5. Fit replacements from current stock and record the lot numbers.
6. Restore the unit, record the new differential pressure and remove the tag.
7. Where the zone includes records storage or cold storage, check the humidity and temperature logs for that zone at completion and note the readings in the filter change log.

A gas-phase bed is sampled for breakthrough at the midpoint of its cycle. A failed sample brings the change forward to the next working day.

The filter change log is retained for five years. Logs for units serving seal-capable zones are retained for the life of the unit.

## Annex A: Seal-Capable Zones, Facilities

:::redact{tier=2 auth="FE" ref="OSC-ASR-THR-02"}
The following zones are seal-capable at every ALPHA-class facility: the refuge chamber on each level; the operations floor and its equipment rooms; the communications equipment rooms; the power plant control room and switchgear; the clinic; and berthing.

Berthing is seal-capable only to the number of berths provisioned under the reserves basis for the facility. Berthing above that number is supplied from the general air handling units, carries no gas-phase beds, and is not on the seal list.

Records stacks and cold storage, at facilities that have them, are seal-capable on their own units and are not shed when the facility reduces load.

The surface cover facility, the lift and shaft heads and the vehicle inspection areas are not seal-capable. They are isolated at the first blast door when the facility goes to SEALED.
:::

## Annex B: Seal-Capable Zones, Tunnels

:::redact{compartment=KESTREL auth="FE" ref="OSC-TRN-ENG"}
Each terminal platform level is seal-capable to the platform seal doors. Running tunnels are not seal-capable between portals; ventilation shaft houses are fitted with gas-phase beds on the standard cycle so that a tunnel can be purged before a movement after a seal state is lifted.

Inter-hub rail segment portal seal doors are held open in revenue service and closed on any seal state at either terminal.

The portal seals of a segment not in revenue service are held in the SEALED state at both terminals. The shaft house units on such a segment remain on the shortened cycle, and their changes are made under possession, combined with the monthly portal seal walk-down where possible.
:::
