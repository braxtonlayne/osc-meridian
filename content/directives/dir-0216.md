## Purpose

This directive governs the issue, wear and exchange of personal dosimeters, the keeping of dose records, and the action taken when a dosimeter is lost or a reading exceeds an investigation level.

## Applicability

This directive applies at facilities with reactor plant to every person who enters a reactor space, including visitors under escort and personnel present for a surveillance test.

## Policy

No person enters a reactor space without a dosimetry badge issued to them by name. Badges are not shared, lent or worn by anyone but the person to whom they are issued.

Badges are worn on the chest, outside clothing, whenever the wearer is in a reactor space, and are left on the badge board at the health physics window at all other times. A badge is not taken out of the facility.

Badges are exchanged monthly on the exchange day posted at the health physics window. A badge not returned by the end of the exchange day is reported to the division head of the person to whom it was issued.

Doses are recorded against the personnel number. The administrative control level is 1,000 mrem in a calendar year. A reading above 100 mrem in a single month is investigated by the site medical officer before the individual next enters a reactor space.

Dose records are kept for the working life of the individual and for thirty years after. They are not destroyed on transfer, on leaving a facility's posted strength, or on any change of status.

## Responsibilities

The site medical officer runs the health physics window, issues and exchanges badges, sends badges for reading, keeps the dose record and conducts investigations.

The senior reactor operator ensures that no person enters a reactor space without a badge and that the entry log is kept.

Division heads ensure their personnel return badges on exchange day.

## Procedures

A lost dosimeter is reported to the health physics window at once. The individual does not re-enter a reactor space until a replacement is issued. The dose for the period of the lost badge is assessed by the site medical officer from the entry log and the area survey record, and entered in the dose record as an assessed dose.

A dosimeter that has been damaged, dropped in water or left in a reactor space is handed in at the health physics window and treated as lost if it cannot be read.

Each person may see their own dose record at the health physics window on request. Copies are not issued.
